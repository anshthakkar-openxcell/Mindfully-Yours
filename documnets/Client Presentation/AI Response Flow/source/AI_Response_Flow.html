How the AI produces a reply

The flow from a user's message, through your Knowledge Base and therapist conversations, the database and the AI, to the reply the user receives.

Prepared for the Mindfully Yours clinical and product team  |  Version 1.0

The short answer

  1. Your therapists' conversations come first. Every message is compared, by meaning, with the approved therapist replies in the library. When one fits, the user gets your therapist's reply, as written or lightly reworded. The AI language model writes a reply on its own only when nothing in the library fits, and then it stays short and careful.
  2. Safety is checked first, every time. A message that matches a red flag gets your team's approved script, word for word. No AI writing is involved.
  3. One PostgreSQL database does two jobs. The ordinary tables (the RDS) hold the text, rules and scripts; the vector search (pgvector) inside the same database finds the closest match by meaning. They are not two separate systems.
  4. Users in other languages get the same treatment: the message is translated for the safety check and the search, and the reply comes back in their own language.

How the Knowledge Base gets into the database

Your team's files are read once, checked, saved, given meaning vectors and approved. Rows that could be unsafe or unfinished are kept in the database but are never searchable (no vector, not approved): crisis conversations (the safety check owns those), unfinished text, content on governance hold, and one-word messages that would match anything. The loads so far cover the clinical sheets, the question banks, the self-care tools and the therapist conversations.

Where does the reply come from?

There are five possible sources for a reply. They are tried in this order, and the first one that applies is used. The closer the match, the more the reply is purely your therapists' words.

1
Approved safety script, word for wordA red flag is detected in the message
Your team's words only
2
The therapist's reply, exactly as writtenA very close match in the library (a strong match)
Your therapists' words only. The AI is not used.
3
The therapists' replies, lightly rewordedA fair match (a partial match)
The AI sees a few close therapist examples and may add nothing new
4
A therapist-written clarifying questionNothing matches, and nothing else can guide the reply
Your therapists' words only
5
A short, careful AI replyStill nothing that fits
AI language model, with self-care advice if found; suggests a practitioner when unsure

What "close" means

Every message and every therapist conversation is turned into a meaning vector. "How close" is a similarity score between the two. The cut-offs between strong, partial and none are provisional starting values, set conservatively so that "exactly as written" is only used for very clear matches. They are meant to be set with your therapists, using a sample of messages they mark as "fits" or "does not fit".

Always true

  • The safety check runs before everything else.
  • The AI never decides whether something is a crisis.
  • Every reply is recorded with which of the five sources produced it, so the share coming from your therapists can be reported.

What the AI language model sees (the RAG step)

RAG means retrieve, then generate: first the system retrieves the relevant material from the database; only then is the AI asked to write a reply using that material. The AI is never asked to work from memory alone.

Always given to the AI

  • The persona and tone (warm, calm, non-clinical)
  • The language to reply in
  • A short checklist of what to quietly notice (the Clinical Markers)
  • The last few messages of this conversation
  • The user's message

Plus one kind of retrieved material

  • Close therapist examples (a few) when the match is partial
  • Self-care advice found by searching for the user's situation
  • Therapist probing guidance for the closest pattern
  • A gentle booking suggestion, once concern has persisted and its duration and impact are clear
  • A therapist follow-up question for a possible concern

The AI is never asked to

  • decide whether a message is a crisis (the safety check does that)
  • add advice to a matched therapist reply
  • work from other users' conversations
  • diagnose, or name a clinical condition to the user

Checked before the user sees it

When the AI rewords therapist replies, the result is checked: it must reuse the therapists' own wording, add no numbers or links, and stay about as short. If it drifts, the therapist's original reply is sent instead.

A real example of what the AI is shown (shortened), for a partial match

Real examples from our therapist team, closest first:
1. When someone said: "I miss my home, my family and my friends. I don't know anyone in this city..."
    the therapist replied: "It sounds like you feel homesick and lonely in the new place. How can I help you?"
2. When someone said: "I've recently moved to a new city for work and I'm really struggling..."
    the therapist replied: "hey thanks for sharing that. Can you tell me more about the hardest part for you so far?"
Write ONE reply, using ONLY the ideas, wording and questions already in the therapist replies. Do NOT add advice or facts that are not in them.
The user just said: "I just moved to a new city for college and I feel so alone..."
The AI wrote: "It sounds like you feel alone in the new place. How can I help you?"
